The math behind it

Four equations, one spike

The voltage changes as charged ions flow through three kinds of channels. Each channel's opening is its own small equation, so the whole neuron is four linked differential equations, solved here 100,000 times per simulated second.

C·dV/dt = I − gNam³h(V − ENa) − gKn⁴(V − EK) − gL(V − EL)
Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley found this in 1952 from experiments on the squid giant axon, and won the 1963 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Why it matters in medicine

Same channels, real cases

  • Local anaesthetics block Na⁺ channels so pain signals can't fire
  • Many epilepsy drugs calm over-active Na⁺ channels
  • Multiple sclerosis damages myelin and slows signals
